Workload policy, applied the same way to every faculty member.

AWMS turns your collective agreement and department practice into versioned rules. It calculates each person's annual load from your course data, and closes the year with a statement that faculty acknowledge and chairs sign off.

What it replaces

The workload spreadsheet that gets copied every term, with formulas nobody fully trusts and no record of who changed what.

Policy held in memory. Enrolment bonuses, coordinator credits and course releases applied a little differently by each administrator.

Per-term judgements that make anyone with a release or a heavy Winter term look overloaded or underloaded.

Disputes by email, where the reply is agreed but the official record never changes.

One system for the whole workload year

Your policy, written as rules you can read

Each rule states when it applies and what it adds or removes. Conditions can use enrolment, rank, FTE, role and release history. Rules are published as a version for each term, so last year's numbers keep last year's rules.

A year-end record everyone has agreed to

Staff publish each person's year as a frozen statement. Faculty review it and acknowledge it, or report a problem. Once acknowledged, the chair signs off.

  • Sign-off is blocked while a dispute is open, or if the figures have changed since publishing.
  • A change after sign-off needs a new revision. The earlier one stays in the history.
  • Every publication and sign-off is recorded in the audit log.

Disputes that end in a corrected record

A faculty member flags the line they disagree with, whether that's a course, a release, a service role or the annual total. Staff go straight to that line in the editor and make the change.

The reply automatically states what changed and by how much, so nobody has to compare two versions of a spreadsheet.

Configured to your institution, not the other way round

AWMS separates the facts it reads from your systems from the decisions your department makes, so neither overwrites the other.

Your units
Load rules and units per FCE are set by discipline and stream, including teaching and tenure streams.
Your course data
Courses, sections and enrolment come from a read-only mirror of your student information system, or from spreadsheet import.
Your academic year
Fall, Winter and Summer roll up into one annual figure, which is what targets and thresholds are judged on.
Your people
Separate views for faculty, staff, chairs, associate chairs, deans and administrators, each limited to their own scope.
Your exceptions
Release types with caps and account rules, leave with FTE, teaching credits, supervision and approved adjustments.
Your reporting
Annual workload letters, dean's reports, history and data export.

Questions institutions ask

Does AWMS replace our student information system?+

No. It reads course, section and enrolment data and never writes back. Assignments, releases and workload decisions live in AWMS.

Can it follow our collective agreement?+

Load rules are set per discipline and stream, and policy is written as conditions and effects. Part of implementation is working through your agreement and checking the rules against real cases.

What happens when a faculty member disagrees with a number?+

They flag the specific line from their dashboard. Staff correct it in the editor, the reply records what changed, and the statement can't be signed off while the dispute is open.

How are releases and leave handled?+

They lower the person's target rather than counting as work. Someone on a half-year leave who teaches the reduced load shows as on target, not underloaded.

Where is it hosted?+

We'll go through hosting and data residency with your IT and privacy offices as part of the first conversation.
